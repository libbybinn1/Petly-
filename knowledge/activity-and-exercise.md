# Activity Levels and Daily Time Commitment

## Matching energy, not just hours
A mismatch in either direction causes problems. An under-exercised
high-energy animal becomes destructive and anxious. A low-energy or senior
animal pushed into an active household becomes stressed, sore and withdrawn.

## Indicative daily time
These are working guidelines for structured attention: walks, training, play
and handling, excluding time merely in the same room.

- Low activity: about one hour daily. Most cats, rabbits, senior animals,
  brachycephalic breeds and small rodents.
- Moderate activity: about two and a half hours. Most adult dogs of balanced
  temperament, active cats and social birds.
- High activity: four or more hours. Working and herding breeds, young large
  dogs, huskies and malamutes.

## Time available is a hard limiting factor
An adopter with one hour a day cannot meet a four-hour animal's needs no
matter how willing they are. Shortfalls of this size should reduce a match
score sharply rather than marginally, because the gap is not recoverable
through goodwill.

## Working households
A household empty for nine hours is unsuitable for animals prone to
separation distress, particularly young dogs and parrots. Cats, rabbits kept
in bonded pairs and small rodents tolerate absence far better.
