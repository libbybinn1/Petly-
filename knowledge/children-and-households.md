# Compatibility with Children

## Age matters more than the presence of children
Households with children under about twelve need animals with a demonstrated
tolerance for unpredictable handling, noise and sudden movement. Older
children can be taught to read animal body language and to respect an
animal's retreat space.

## Animals not suited to young children
An animal recorded as not good with children should not be placed with young
children even under promised supervision. Supervision fails in ordinary
moments, and the consequences fall on both child and animal. Where the
animal's history with children is unknown, treat the unknown as the risky
case rather than assuming it is fine.

## Small animals are not automatically child-friendly
Hamsters, and particularly the smaller dwarf species, are fragile, nocturnal
and stressed by handling. They are frequently mis-sold as starter pets for
young children. Guinea pigs and rabbits tolerate gentle handling better but
still require supervision and a calm approach.

## Practical guidance
Give the animal an accessible retreat the children cannot enter. Teach
children to approach rather than chase. Never leave a very young child alone
with any animal, however reliable it has been.
