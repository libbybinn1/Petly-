# Introducing Animals to Existing Pets

## Some animals must be the only pet
An animal recorded as not good with other animals should be placed as an only
pet. Resource guarding, predation risk and chronic stress do not reliably
resolve with time, and a failed introduction can injure both animals.

## Predator and prey species must not be mixed
Cats and dogs with a high prey drive should not be placed into homes with
rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters or small birds, even where separate housing is
promised. Proximity alone is a chronic stressor for prey species.

## Species that prefer company
Guinea pigs are highly social and suffer when housed alone; they should be
adopted in pairs or into a home with an existing compatible guinea pig.
Rabbits generally do better bonded. Budgerigars are flock birds and should not
be kept singly.

Syrian hamsters are the opposite: they are solitary and will fight, sometimes
fatally, if housed together.

## Introductions take weeks
Scent exchange first, then visual contact through a barrier, then short
supervised meetings on neutral ground. Rushing this is the most common cause
of a failed multi-pet placement.
