# Dogs: Characteristics and Requirements

## Herding breeds
Border Collies, Australian Shepherds and similar breeds were bred to work all
day and solve problems. Without a job they invent one, typically herding
children, chasing vehicles or dismantling the house. They need four or more
hours of combined physical and mental activity and suit experienced owners
with space. They are poor apartment animals.

## Northern breeds
Huskies and Malamutes are independent, vocal, powerful and expert escapers.
Recall is unreliable by temperament rather than by training failure. They need
secure high fencing and an owner who accepts they may never be reliably
off-lead. They tolerate heat badly.

## Retrievers and gundogs
Labradors and similar breeds are typically patient, food-motivated and good
with children, which makes them common family choices. They need real daily
exercise and are prone to obesity where that is not provided.

## Terriers
Small in size, large in drive. Jack Russells and similar need far more
exercise than their size suggests and often have a strong prey drive, making
them unsuitable for homes with small pets.

## Companion breeds
Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, Bichons and similar were bred for company
rather than work. They suit apartments and lower-activity households but are
prone to separation distress and should not be left alone all day.

## Greyhounds
Frequently misunderstood. Retired racing greyhounds sprint briefly and then
sleep for most of the day. They are typically calm, quiet and well suited to
apartment living despite their size, though many have a high prey drive.

## Brachycephalic breeds
Bulldogs, Pugs and Boxers to a lesser degree cannot regulate temperature or
sustain strenuous exercise. Short, cool walks only. They should never be
matched to an adopter seeking a running or hiking companion.
