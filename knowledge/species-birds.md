# Birds: Parrots, Finches, Canaries and Doves

## Birds are not a single category
The difference between a zebra finch and a cockatoo is larger than the
difference between a hamster and a dog. Lifespans run from five years to over
seventy, noise from a barely audible peep to a call measured above 100
decibels, and handling tolerance from "never" to "expects to sit on you all
evening". Matching a household to "a bird" is meaningless; the species is the
whole question.

## Budgerigars and cockatiels
The two most commonly kept parrots and the most reasonable starting point.
Budgerigars live five to ten years, cockatiels fifteen to twenty. Both are
flock birds and should not be kept singly unless the owner can provide
several hours of direct company every day. Cockatiels make loud contact calls
when separated from their flock, which in a home means when their person
leaves the room. Both need daily out-of-cage time and a cage wide enough to
open both wings.

## Larger parrots
African Greys live forty to sixty years, Amazons and Eclectus thirty or more,
and cockatoos routinely exceed fifty. They are as intelligent as a young
child and suffer accordingly when under-stimulated: screaming,
feather-plucking and self-mutilation are the standard consequences, not
unusual ones. Cockatoos are the loudest and the most frequently rehomed
parrot, and they also produce a fine feather dust that affects people with
respiratory conditions. Placing a large parrot requires an experienced home
and a written plan for who takes the bird if the adopter dies first.

## Conures and parakeets
Green-cheeked conures are small, affectionate and comparatively quiet. Sun
conures are similar in size and among the loudest birds kept in homes; they
are unsuitable for attached housing regardless of how much the adopter likes
them. Indian Ringnecks and Quaker parrots are clever talkers living
twenty-five to thirty years; Quakers are restricted or prohibited in some
jurisdictions because escaped birds establish wild colonies.

## Canaries and finches
Kept for their song and their movement rather than for handling. Canaries
live ten to fifteen years and only the males sing. Zebra, society and
Gouldian finches live five to ten years and must be kept in groups; a single
finch is a welfare failure. Finches need horizontal flight space rather than
climbing height, so a tall narrow parrot cage suits them poorly. Gouldian
finches are the most delicate and need a consistently warm room.

## Doves
Diamond and ringneck doves are gentle, undemanding and among the quietest
birds available, cooing softly at dawn and dusk. They live ten to twenty
years, prefer company of their own kind, and suit adopters who want to watch
a bird rather than interact with one.

## Care facts that apply to every bird
An all-seed diet causes obesity, fatty liver disease and vitamin A
deficiency; the base of the diet should be formulated pellets with fresh
vegetables. Avocado, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol and salt are toxic. Fumes
from overheated non-stick cookware kill birds within minutes, and aerosols,
scented candles and cigarette smoke cause chronic respiratory disease.

Birds conceal illness until they can no longer compensate. A bird sitting
fluffed and quiet at the bottom of the cage is an emergency, not a bird
having a quiet day.
