# Space and Housing Requirements

## Apartments without outdoor access
Apartments suit small and some medium animals. The limiting factor is rarely
floor area but the absence of a secure place to toilet, run and decompress.
Animals with high exercise needs housed in apartments without a committed
daily outdoor routine commonly develop destructive behaviour, excessive
vocalisation and stress-related illness.

Small rodents, rabbits kept in a secure indoor pen, cats and small calm dogs
adapt well. Large working breeds do not, regardless of how devoted the owner is.

## Houses with a garden
A secure garden raises a home's effective capacity by roughly one size band.
It does not replace walks: a garden is a toilet and a decompression space, not
exercise. Fencing must be checked for the specific animal; a determined
terrier or a husky will exploit gaps a labrador would ignore.

## Farms and rural properties
Suit large, high-energy and working animals. Risks run the other way: livestock
exposure, road access, and predators for small animals kept outdoors.

## Space is a welfare threshold, not a preference
Placing a large animal in a small home without outdoor access is a welfare
problem rather than a suboptimal match. Organizations should treat it as a
hard constraint that cannot be offset by enthusiasm or experience elsewhere.
