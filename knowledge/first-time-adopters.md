# Guidance for First-Time Adopters

## Good first matches
Adult animals with established, documented temperaments. An adult cat of
balanced temperament, a calm adult dog past adolescence, or a bonded pair of
guinea pigs are all reasonable first placements. The animal's behaviour is
already known rather than speculative.

## Poor first matches
Puppies and kittens, which are demanding and whose adult temperament is not
yet known. Working and herding breeds. Animals with ongoing medical needs.
Anxious or under-socialised animals requiring behavioural rehabilitation.
Parrots of any size.

## The adolescent gap
Most returns to shelters happen when an animal is between six and eighteen
months old, as the manageable young animal becomes a full-sized one testing
boundaries. First-time adopters should be told this explicitly before
adopting a young animal.

## Cost
Adopters routinely underestimate ongoing cost: food, preventative veterinary
care, vaccination, insurance, grooming and boarding. Animals with special
needs cost substantially more. A placement that fails financially fails the
animal.

## Time to settle
Most animals need several weeks to show their real personality, and anxious
animals need months. Judging a placement in the first fortnight is premature.
