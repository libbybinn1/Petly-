# Rabbits, Rodents and Birds

## Rabbits
Rabbits are not low-maintenance starter pets. They need a large secure pen or
free-roaming space, unlimited hay, daily handling to stay tame, and
vaccination. They are easily frightened and can injure themselves escaping a
perceived threat. Most do better bonded with another rabbit. Litter training
is achievable. Lifespan is eight to twelve years.

Giant breeds such as Flemish Giants need far more space than adopters expect,
closer to the requirements of a medium dog than a small pet.

## Guinea pigs
Highly social and should not be kept alone. They are vocal, gentle and
tolerate handling better than most rodents, which makes them reasonable for
supervised families. They need vitamin C in the diet, as they cannot
synthesise it themselves. Lifespan five to seven years.

## Hamsters
Nocturnal and solitary. Syrian hamsters must be housed alone and will fight
fatally if not. Dwarf species are faster and harder to handle. They are
frequently mismatched to young children who want a daytime pet. Lifespan two
to three years.

## Budgerigars and cockatiels
Flock birds that should not be kept singly. They need out-of-cage time, mental
enrichment and daily interaction. Cockatiels whistle and call loudly, which
suits some households poorly. Lifespan ten to twenty years.

## African Greys and larger parrots
A decades-long commitment comparable to raising a child. Extremely intelligent
and prone to feather-plucking, screaming and self-harm when under-stimulated
or rehomed repeatedly. They need experienced owners, daily interaction and
stable routines. Lifespan can exceed fifty years.
